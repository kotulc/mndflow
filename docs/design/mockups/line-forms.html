Line forms

Round two. The swatch stays as a line's icon. Labels no longer sit on the stroke: a line keeps its ends visible as anchors on the card walls, its name sits beside it, and multiplicity, role and the rest come on hover. The last section proposes one colour grammar for rows, chips, cards, tiles, tags and lines.

1 · A line's icon is its swatch

Decided. The relation type's swatch is a short stroke in the type's colour and dash, with its end marks. It is the line's icon everywhere: diagram, map tile, chip, explorer row, tray and hover card.

2 · In a diagram: anchors visible, details on hover

Hover a line (anywhere along it) to light it, show its end words at the anchors, and open its hover card. Nothing ever sits on top of the stroke.

B · anchors + name beside the line
C · anchors only; the name comes with room
  • Anchors are where a line meets a card, drawn on the wall in the type's colour: the diamond of an owner, the head of a direction, a short bar for a plain end. They are the line's seats. They hide when too small to read, as seats do.
  • The name sits beside the stroke, above a level leg and to the right of an upright one, without a background box, so the line runs unbroken under nothing.
  • Ends (multiplicity, role) appear at the anchors only while the line is hovered or picked, and in its hover card.
  • Levels, from the line's length on screen. B: stroke → anchors → name. C: stroke → anchors, with the name only at the largest level.

3 · On the map: a relation type's chip

A relation chip gathers its lines by place. Each tile is the swatch plus the line's name, and an unnamed line names its pair. Tiles wrap inside their place and never run past the chip.

4 · One colour grammar

Today colour arrives as fills, edges, dots and strokes all at once, at different strengths, so a tag can look like a kind and a model looks selected. The proposal: grounds stay neutral, and colour is only ever a thin mark, as on the explorer rows you liked. Each role owns one shape of mark, so the same hue in two roles never reads alike.
